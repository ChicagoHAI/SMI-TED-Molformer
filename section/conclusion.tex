\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Taken together, the attention and probing results suggest that chemical information is not represented uniformly across mechanisms or layers. Attention exhibits weak and highly selective alignment with molecular geometry, while probing and causal intervention show that chemically meaningful properties are strongly represented within the residual stream but become usable at substantially different depths across models. More broadly, our results move beyond asking whether chemical information is present toward understanding how it is internally organized and used during computation.

\para{Future directions.}
Several directions remain open for understanding chemical representations in molecular foundation models. While probing identifies which properties are recoverable, it does not explain how chemical features are organized across layers or whether they occupy shared subspaces, motivating future work on representation geometry, feature overlap, and superposition using methods such as sparse autoencoders or representation similarity analysis. Our interventions further show when a representation becomes causally usable, but not how later components consume or combine this information into more global molecular reasoning, suggesting future analysis of attention circuits and residual-stream interactions. More broadly, although \molm and \smi achieve similar downstream performance, they differ substantially in how and when chemical information becomes usable, raising the broader question of how pretraining objectives and architectural choices shape internal scientific representations.
